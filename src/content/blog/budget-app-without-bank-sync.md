## Why people leave bank sync

Three things lie behind the question. Each is linked to the organisation's own
document, and each was read on 13 September 2026.

**The app, not the bank, holds your budget.** In October 2023, Intuit's Credit
Karma [announced](https://web.archive.org/web/20231107195404/https://www.creditkarma.com/about/releases/intuit-credit-karma-welcomes-all-minters)
that it was folding Intuit's budgeting app, Mint, into Credit Karma.
[Mint's own help page](https://web.archive.org/web/20231109015511/https://mint.intuit.com/support/en-us/help-article/export-transactions/need-know-mint-moving-credit-karma/L30uqxD8W_US_en_US)
told users they could carry over account balances, net worth history and three
years of transactions, and that Credit Karma "doesn't offer the ability to set
monthly and category budgets".

**The connection usually runs through a third party.** In the table above,
Goodbudget's and Skwad's bank connections both go through Plaid. In July 2022 a
federal court
[approved a $58 million settlement](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-4_20-cv-03056/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-4_20-cv-03056-4.pdf)
of class actions in which the plaintiffs alleged that Plaid "uses consumers'
banking login credentials to harvest and sell detailed financial data without
their consent". Plaid denied liability. The settlement required changes to its
practices, among them "minimizing the data Plaid stores from users' financial
accounts".

**Handing over a bank password is being phased out, slowly.** When the US
Consumer Financial Protection Bureau
[finalized a rule](https://web.archive.org/web/20251229115723/https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-finalizes-personal-financial-data-rights-rule-to-boost-competition-protect-privacy-and-give-families-more-choice-in-financial-services/)
in October 2024, it called screen scraping "a still common but risky practice
that typically involves consumers providing their account passwords to third
parties". Its own figures show the practice shrinking: among the aggregators it
collected data from, screen scraping fell from 80 percent of access attempts in
2019 to 50 percent in 2022
([89 FR 90838](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/11/18/2024-25079/required-rulemaking-on-personal-financial-data-rights)).
A federal court
[stayed the rule's compliance dates](https://web.archive.org/web/20260824202244/https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/other-applicable-requirements/personal-financial-data-rights/)
in October 2025, and the CFPB
[is reconsidering it](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/22/2025-16139/personal-financial-data-rights-reconsideration).

## What you give up without it

Nothing arrives on its own. Every transaction is one you typed, or one that came
in a file you downloaded from your bank and imported yourself, so a balance is
as current as the last time you did that.

What you get in exchange is the other side of the same fact: nobody else is
logging in to your bank on your behalf, there is no connection to break or
re-authorise, and nothing about your accounts reaches a company whose business
is holding that data.

## How the seven differ

**Whether they can connect at all.** Envy, Fundkeep and Pennies never do.
Actual Budget, Goodbudget, MoneyCoach and Skwad can, and the table says on
which terms: Goodbudget and MoneyCoach keep it for a paid plan, Skwad for its
LINK plan, and Actual Budget for a data provider you set up yourself.
Goodbudget connects only to US banks, and MoneyCoach only to European ones.

**How you pay.** Actual Budget is free and open source. Goodbudget and
MoneyCoach have free tiers with paid plans above them, and Pennies and Skwad
sell subscriptions. Envy sells one in-app purchase, and Fundkeep is one
purchase with a free trial.

**Where they run.** Envy, Fundkeep, MoneyCoach and Pennies are made for Apple
devices.
Actual Budget, Goodbudget and Skwad also run on the web, and Goodbudget and
Skwad on Android.

## Where Fundkeep fits

It is ours, so read this part accordingly. Fundkeep is envelope budgeting for
iPhone, iPad and Mac, bought once. It never connects to a bank: you type
transactions or import the CSV file your bank gives you, and see every row
before anything is saved. It does not run on Android, Windows or the web, and a
budget is one person's, kept in step across that person's own devices.
